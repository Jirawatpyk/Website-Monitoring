## D-073 — How the monthly report counts (S20)

**Context.** The Reports and ReportPDF boards show counts ("3 outages, 1 open"), a target ("Target 99.9% met") and daily cells, and spec §12 / §14.12 say which incidents a client sees. Several readings were open, and plan Q2 (the renderer) was open.

**Decision (Tao, 22 Nov 2026, on Claude's recommendations).**

1. **Renderer:** HTML rendered by headless Chromium on Vercel (`@sparticuz/chromium`). Fonts: AURA's own bundled files (`@jirawatpyk/aura-tokens/fonts`) inlined as `data:` URLs — Inter for text, Fraunces for the client name and the DxT mark, JetBrains Mono for the eyebrow, table headers and report ID, Noto Sans Thai behind each (amended 22 Nov: no `@fontsource` package; the display and mono faces after spec-checker, as the board draws them). The page is an HTML string template with every value escaped (Next 16 refuses `react-dom/server` in app code). The spike: React PDF dropped Thai tone marks (ติ้ง → ติง, ตั๋ว → ตัว, น้ำ → นำ) and the degraded hatch; Chromium kept both, repeated the header, numbered pages and kept each incident on one page (70 KB, 0.3–0.5 s locally).
2. **An outage** is an incident with a DOWN drop in the month: counted once however often it dropped, its DOWN minutes (clipped to the month) summed, as the weekly summary does (S14 `reportIncident`). A manual (hand-check) DOWN is an outage. An incident that was a PROBLEM and became DOWN is an outage.
3. **A degraded** is an incident with no DOWN drop in the month whose PROBLEM is client-visible: slow, key page, regional, intermittent (spec §12, §6). SSL, domain, TLS chain, keyword, redirect, page change and blocked-unverified PROBLEMs are internal: not listed, not counted, no day cell.
4. **False alarms:** an incident resolved with root cause "False alarm (monitor)" is left out of the report: not listed, and its DOWN minutes don't count against the report's uptime. The site wasn't down. The internal pages keep counting every DOWN minute (spec §5), so a report can read higher than Site detail for that month.
5. **Target:** each site's own tier target (`TIER_CONFIG`: Tier 1 99.95%, Tier 2 99.9%, Tier 3 99.5%); a client's "Target met" means every one of its sites met its own. The board's "Target 99.9%" was sample copy.
6. **A site watched for part of the month** counts from when monitoring started (D-031); the days before are drawn empty.
7. **Root-cause gate:** a report can't go out while a client-visible incident in it has no root cause (or "Unknown"); internal-only ones don't block.
8. **Language:** the report's labels are English; the staff's "What happened" text appears as written. A Thai template for `th` clients is later.
9. **Minutes (engine review, 22 Nov):** an incident's minutes are whole clock minutes (D-033 `clockMinutes`, at least 1) of what the client sees, clipped to the month and outside maintenance — the same number the Incident page and the weekly summary print. A site's and a client's Downtime is those lines added up, so the page adds up. Uptime and "Target met" are worked out on the exact time, as the internal pages do (spec §5), and rounded only for display. A PROBLEM drop is split by its causes (`new_cause` / `cause_back`, D-067), so only a client-visible stretch is degraded; "open" means a client-visible stretch is still going at the end of that month.
10. **Response figures (db review, 22 Nov):** "Typical response" and "Slowest 5%" are the median of the month's daily all-location `p50_ms` / `p95_ms` rollups weighted by `checks` — the rule Site detail uses for 30 days (`weightedMedian`, moved to `@dxt/shared`), so the report and Site detail agree for the same month. A client's figure is the same median over all its sites' days. The month is read in one query (day buckets, primary monitors, `location is null`).
11. **Kept (Claude, on the recommended option):** monthly drafts and their PDFs are kept forever, like incidents: once hour rollups go at 90 days and day rollups at 2 years, a past month's figures exist only in `client_reports.figures` (about 200 rows and 14 MB a year). A file is never deleted without its row.
12. **Stopped periods (Claude, on the recommended option; engine review question):** a stretch when a site's monitoring was stopped (Stop → Draft, read from the `audit_log` row of each `is_active` change) isn't watched: it leaves the window and the minutes, and a day stopped throughout is drawn empty. `monitoring_started_at` alone would count it as 100% up.
13. **Stored versus live (Claude, on the recommended option, 22 Nov):** a closed month's page row and PDF both read its stored draft, so they always agree (Done-when 1). The drafts cron runs every 10 minutes (an unchanged draft costs one read; amended 22 Nov for §42 item 8, "refreshes on its own within a few minutes"). From 06:00 on the 1st it makes each missing draft, and after that it re-makes a draft whose figures changed (a root cause added, a false alarm marked) until S20b's send freezes it. A change shows on the page and in the PDF within about 10 minutes. The running month is always worked out on demand and never stored. The GET route never writes.
14. **Who opens the PDF (Claude, from PRD §3, 22 Nov):** every staff role sees the Reports page (§42), but opening or downloading a PDF follows the role matrix row "Download uptime reports": Admin, Responder, Stakeholder. A Viewer sees the figures; Preview / Open PDF are disabled with the reason, and the route refuses a Viewer (403). A live site with no `monitoring_started_at` (set live by a script) counts from when it was added, as Site detail does (`watchedSince`); a Draft that never started gets no report.
